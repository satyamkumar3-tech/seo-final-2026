Your correct bangle size is the smallest inner diameter that passes comfortably over the widest part of your grouped hand without forcing, pinching, or slipping off later. The easiest method is to measure the inner diameter of a bangle that already fits; if you do not have one, measure around your closed hand at the widest knuckles and use the conversion chart below.

TL;DR: Measure a comfortable bangle across its inner edges, or bring your thumb toward your little finger and measure around the widest part of the hand. Match the result to the Indian size chart. For a closed bangle, do not use wrist circumference alone. If you fall between two listed sizes, select the next larger closed-bangle size and confirm the individual product guide.

Bangle size chart in mm, cm and inches

The BlueStone Bangle Measuring Guide and the Gem and Jewellery Skill Council of India retail training handbook use the standard inner-diameter progression below. Values are rounded for practical measuring, so retain millimetres until you have chosen the closest size.

Indian bangle sizes describe the approximate inner diameter of a circular bangle, with labels progressing from 2-2 to 2-14 and then 3-0.

Inner diameter is the clear distance between the inside edges through the centre, while inner circumference is the distance around that opening.

Use diameter when copying a well-fitting bangle and circumference when measuring the widest part of your grouped hand.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. A 2-2 bangle has an inner diameter of about 54.0 mm and an inner circumference of about 169.4 mm.
  2. A 2-4 bangle has an inner diameter of about 57.2 mm and an inner circumference of about 179.6 mm.
  3. A 2-6 bangle has an inner diameter of about 60.3 mm and an inner circumference of about 189.5 mm.
  4. A 2-8 bangle has an inner diameter of about 63.5 mm and an inner circumference of about 199.4 mm.
  5. A 2-10 bangle has an inner diameter of about 66.7 mm and an inner circumference of about 209.3 mm.
  6. A 2-12 bangle has an inner diameter of about 69.9 mm and an inner circumference of about 219.5 mm.

How to measure bangle size using a bangle you own

This is usually the simplest method because it copies a fit that already works. Accuracy depends on choosing a comfortable, undistorted reference and measuring only the clear opening through its true centre.

Choose a round bangle that passes over your hand comfortably and does not fall off during normal movement.

Place it flat on a firm surface beside a ruler marked in millimetres so the circle is not tilted or distorted.

Measure straight through the centre from one inner edge to the opposite inner edge, excluding the metal thickness.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. Repeat the measurement at a second angle to catch an oval shape, a bent bangle, or an off-centre reading.
  2. Record the inner diameter in millimetres before converting it to centimetres or inches because millimetres reduce rounding error.
  3. Compare the diameter with the chart and check the nearest listed Indian size rather than reading the outer diameter.
  4. If the bangle is hinged or oval, use it only as a like-for-like reference for another design with similar construction.
  5. Do not copy a bangle that is already uncomfortably tight, very loose, visibly bent, or difficult to remove.
  6. Photograph the ruler across the inner opening if you need to discuss the result with a store or customer-care team.

How to measure your hand for bangle size at home

Use the hand method when you do not own a suitable reference bangle. The measurement must represent the widest shape the closed bangle will pass over, not the narrower wrist where it eventually rests.

Bring all fingers together and move your thumb toward the little finger, copying the hand shape you use to put on a closed bangle.

Wrap a flexible tape around the widest part of the grouped knuckles and thumb joint without squeezing the skin.

If you use a paper strip or string, mark the meeting point and measure that length flat against a millimetre ruler.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. Take the measurement three times and use the largest consistent reading because the bangle must clear the widest point.
  2. Keep the tape level around the hand instead of letting it climb diagonally, which can add unnecessary length.
  3. Match the hand circumference to the closest chart value and choose the next larger listed size if your result falls between sizes.
  4. Measure the dominant and non-dominant hands if you plan to wear the bangle on either side because the readings can differ slightly.
  5. Repeat the process later if swelling, heat, exercise, or pregnancy has temporarily changed the hand measurement.
  6. Use this method for rigid closed bangles, then check product-specific instructions before applying it to hinged or adjustable designs.

Closed, openable and adjustable bangle sizing explained

Bangle construction changes the fitting route. Closed designs slide over the hand, while hinged and adjustable designs can open closer to the wrist. Match the method to the mechanism before converting a number.

A closed bangle has to pass over the thumb joint and knuckles, so its inner opening is chosen from the widest grouped-hand measurement.

A hinged bangle opens before it reaches the hand and may be sized closer to the wrist, but its clasp and inner shape still affect comfort.

An adjustable or cuff-style piece has a different opening mechanism, so the individual product dimensions matter more than a generic closed-bangle chart.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. Do not assume that the same size label has identical fit across circular, oval, hinged, and open designs.
  2. Check whether the listed number refers to inner diameter, inner circumference, wrist circumference, or a brand-specific small, medium, or large label.
  3. For an oval bangle, compare both internal axes with a comfortable oval piece rather than using one round diameter alone.
  4. A secure clasp should close without pinching skin and should not pop open when the wrist bends or rotates.
  5. An open cuff should not be repeatedly bent for fitting unless the product instructions say that controlled adjustment is safe.
  6. When buying online, match the measurement method to the construction shown on the product page before selecting a size.

How should a correctly sized bangle fit?

The correct fit is secure and wearable, not merely possible to put on. Check the entire sequence from passing the hand to moving at the wrist and removing the bangle again.

A closed bangle should pass over the grouped hand with gentle, controlled pressure and without scraping, sharp pain, or forceful twisting.

Once on the wrist, it should move naturally without sliding over the hand during ordinary gestures or washing.

The bangle should not leave deep marks, restrict circulation, catch skin at a hinge, or press painfully against the thumb side of the wrist.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. A very loose bangle that crosses the hand when you point your fingers downward is at risk of slipping off.
  2. A very tight bangle that needs oil, soap, or repeated force every time is not a practical daily fit.
  3. A hinged bangle should open and close smoothly, with enough clearance around the wrist and no pressure from the clasp mechanism.
  4. For a stack, the pieces can move together, but one should not be so tight that it acts as an uncomfortable stopper.
  5. Try the movement you expect in real life, including typing, reaching, carrying a bag, and bending the wrist.
  6. If comfort changes after a few minutes or the hand begins to swell, remove the bangle and reassess the size and construction.

Common bangle size mistakes and how to fix them

Most wrong-size orders begin with a small measurement error or the wrong reference point. A second reading and a construction check can prevent a centimetre, diameter, or wrist measurement from being applied to the wrong product.

Measuring the wrist for a rigid closed bangle ignores the larger hand and knuckles that the bangle must pass over.

Reading the outer diameter of an existing bangle adds the metal thickness and can lead to a size that is too large.

Measuring across an off-centre line produces a shorter chord instead of the true inner diameter.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. Pulling a tape too tightly around the hand understates the clearance needed to slide a rigid bangle on comfortably.
  2. Leaving the fingers spread creates a hand shape that does not match the way a bangle is normally worn.
  3. Rounding centimetres too early can shift the converted result, so record millimetres first and convert only for reference.
  4. Copying a bent or oval bangle with one measurement can hide the difference between its two internal axes.
  5. Assuming every brand uses the same small, medium, or large conversion ignores product-specific tolerances and construction.
  6. Ordering for a gift from wrist appearance alone is guesswork; use a well-fitting bangle discreetly or choose a professional fitting.

Choosing a size for wide, oval and stackable bangles

A size label cannot describe every contact point. Width, profile, internal shape, hinges, stones, and neighbouring bangles all change how a design feels during wear.

A wide bangle covers more skin and can feel firmer than a slim piece even when the inner diameter is the same.

An oval bangle may follow the wrist more closely, but the fit depends on both internal axes and the location of its hinge or clasp.

Stackable bangles need enough shared room to move without trapping skin or becoming difficult to remove as a group.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. Compare a wide design with another wide piece you already wear instead of relying only on a slim bangle reference.
  2. For a closed stack, measure the hand first because every piece must clear the widest knuckle area.
  3. For hinged oval designs, check the internal length and width, opening clearance, clasp position, and product size guide.
  4. A textured edge, projecting motif, or stone setting can change how a bangle feels against neighbouring pieces.
  5. Do not deliberately oversize every stacked piece because an unstable stack can move over the hand and slip off.
  6. When the design is heavy, unusually broad, asymmetric, or expensive to alter, an in-store fitting is the safer choice.

Bangle size buying checklist for online orders

Turn the chart into a safe purchase by recording what you measured and comparing it with the exact listing. Never let a familiar size label replace the product dimensions and construction notes.

Identify whether the product is closed, hinged, openable, adjustable, round, or oval before choosing a measurement method.

Record your measurement in millimetres and note whether it is hand circumference, wrist circumference, or inner diameter.

Compare the recorded value with the exact product size guide rather than relying only on a familiar size label.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. Check the listed inner diameter and shape, not just the outer width or decorative top dimensions.
  2. Confirm whether the design is slim or wide and whether the product page recommends a different fit allowance.
  3. Repeat the measurement three times and resolve any large difference before placing the order.
  4. If using an existing bangle, confirm that it is round, comfortable, and similar in construction to the new design.
  5. Review the current size exchange, return, alteration, and care information on the specific product page before ordering.
  6. Save the measurement and product details so a store or support team can help if the fit needs clarification.

When to visit a BlueStone store for a fitting

Home measurement is useful, but it should not become a guess when the values conflict or the design is complex. A store fitting lets you compare the actual construction, movement, and hand clearance.

Visit a store when repeated home measurements disagree, you fall between sizes, or no comfortable reference bangle is available.

Choose professional fitting for wide, oval, rigid, stone-set, or unusually shaped designs where one conversion number may not tell the whole story.

A store visit is useful when swelling, arthritis, injury, pregnancy, or reduced hand mobility makes sliding a closed bangle difficult.

Keep these quick takeaways handy:

  1. Bring the bangle that fits best and explain whether you prefer a close daily fit or more movement for occasional wear.
  2. Try the exact construction whenever possible because a round closed bangle and a hinged oval bangle can feel different at similar measurements.
  3. Test the clasp, wrist movement, hand clearance, and removal process while help is available.
  4. Ask which measurement is used for the specific product and whether the size can be altered without affecting the design or stone setting.
  5. For a surprise gift, bring a well-fitting bangle or arrange an exchange-friendly selection instead of estimating from age or appearance.
  6. If a bangle becomes stuck, causes numbness, severe swelling, colour change, or pain, stop forcing it and seek appropriate professional help.

Frequently Asked Questions about Bangle Size

How do I know my bangle size at home?

Measure the inner diameter of a comfortable bangle from one inside edge to the other through the centre. If you do not have one, group your fingers with the thumb toward the little finger and measure around the widest part of the hand. Compare the result with the Indian bangle size chart.

What does bangle size 2-6 mean?

Indian bangle size 2-6 corresponds to an inner diameter of about 60.3 mm, or 6.03 cm, and an inner circumference of about 189.5 mm, or 18.95 cm. Use the dimension as a chart reference, then confirm the shape and sizing method for the individual product.

Should I measure my wrist or hand for a closed bangle?

Measure the widest part of your grouped hand for a rigid closed bangle because the piece must pass over the knuckles and thumb joint before reaching the wrist. Wrist circumference is more relevant for some hinged, openable, or adjustable designs, but you should follow the specific product guide.

What should I do if I am between two bangle sizes?

For a rigid closed bangle, the safer home-selection rule is usually to choose the next larger listed size so the piece can clear the widest part of the hand. Confirm this against the product guide because a wide, oval, hinged, or adjustable design may fit differently.

Is bangle size the same as bracelet size?

Not usually. A closed bangle is sized for the widest part of the hand because it has no clasp and must slide over the knuckles. A flexible bracelet or openable design is commonly sized around the wrist. Always check which measurement the seller requests before converting a familiar size.

How tight should a bangle be?

A bangle should pass over the grouped hand with gentle pressure, then move comfortably at the wrist without falling over the hand. It should not pinch, leave deep marks, restrict circulation, or need force every time you remove it. Test ordinary wrist and hand movement before deciding the fit.

Does a wide bangle need a different size?

A wide bangle can feel firmer than a slim bangle with the same inner diameter because it contacts more skin. Compare it with a similar wide design, check the product guidance, and consider an in-store fitting if you are between sizes or the bangle is rigid, heavy, or stone-set.

Can I estimate bangle size for a gift?

A reliable estimate needs a well-fitting bangle that you can measure across its inner diameter. Wrist appearance, age, clothing size, and general build are not dependable substitutes. If you cannot borrow a reference discreetly, choose a professional fitting or confirm the current size exchange options before ordering.

Conclusion

The safest bangle size decision starts with the right measurement for the right construction. Copy the inner diameter of a comfortable bangle when possible. Otherwise, measure the widest part of your grouped hand for a closed design, compare the result with the chart, and confirm the product-specific instructions. If the reading is uncertain or the design is wide, oval, hinged, or difficult to alter, a professional fitting is worth the extra step.
